GeneBox

GeneBox is a molecular biology game that challenges students to create complex gene regulatory networks in different test environments. The game is designed to teach the complexities of gene expression and regulation in the context of high school sciences. In this single-player game, students can play at their own pace, gradually taking on more complex test environments their cells need to survive in.

Game Description


Learning Objectives
- Microbe Biology: The study of microorganisms, which are living organisms too small to be seen with the naked eye.
- Gene Regulatory Networks: students learn the principles of how the genetic code is realized into the function, and the logic behind gene regulation.
- Cell Functions and structures: cell division, pinocytosis, flagellum, apoptosis, photosynthesis, phototaxis, transcription regulation, reaction to starvation, sensing alien microbes, phagocytosis, and cell wall.
- Focusing on the local curriculum, Cells and Molecules of Life, and Organisms and Environment.
- Encourage problem-solving: Revisiting concepts learnt in the classroom and utilising it to solve the different puzzles within the game, fostering critical thinking and decision making.
- Reinforcing learning: By witnessing learned concepts in action within the puzzle and how the functions affect the cell, it creates a longer-lasting impression of what the different functions of a cell are for.
- Inspiration: Through witnessing the cell's micro-actions, participants gain a deeper understanding of microbial biology and are inspired to pursue science in the future.

Themes

- Cause and effect/Problem Solving: After being presented with the problem, players have to identify the correct function and method to solve the problem to exit the level.
- Decision making: Not only do the players have to learn about the different cell functions, but they also have to identify which functions will be beneficial to the current stage of the puzzle and wire the regulatory circuit to activate these functions at the right place, right time, or in response to the correct stimuli.

Gameplay

In this programming simulation game, players control their microbe by editing its gene programming to withstand different test environments. The goal of the game is to ensure the cell colony sustainably grows under different test environments through creating the optimal gene graph. 

Core Mechanics
See Test Environment

Players start the level by assessing the test environment to understand the obstacles their cell colony would navigate through during the simulation. Additionally, players can see the win condition they must pass to succeed the level — to reach a target cell count within an allotted time.

Create Genome Graph

Players open the Genome Panel to edit their Genome Graph by forming links between different genes that determine the cell’s behaviour. During this time, the game is in Edit Mode, in which players can take as long as they want in creating their Genome Graph until they are satisfied.

Run Test Simulations

Once players find their Genome Graph ready, the test begins by clicking the Run Test button. This then activates the game to run the level simulation, and changes the game from Edit Mode to Test Mode. During this, players watch as their cell(s) behave as prescribed within the test environment.

When in Test Mode, the Genome Graph created in the Genome Editor is fixed, in which players cannot change their graph unless they restart the level. Instead, the Genome Panel changes to include a Proteome Graph, which tracks the real-time activity of changing protein concentration levels of selected genes of a highlighted cell.
